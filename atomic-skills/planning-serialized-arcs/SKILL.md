# Planning Serialized Arcs

## Core principle

Plan in **nested horizons**. Lock consequences and tentpoles; keep the corridors between them adaptable.

## Arc hierarchy

Use several scales at once:

- scene / sequence;
- issue or episode;
- short arc, often 3–6 installments;
- season / volume / book;
- saga.

Every level needs its own question, escalation and payoff.

## Story engine

Before planning dozens of installments, state the repeatable engine:

- what repeatedly creates problems;
- why these characters cannot simply leave;
- what resources or institutions generate new dilemmas;
- what changes while the premise remains recognizable.

A premise without an engine runs out of story and compensates with arbitrary twists.

## Conflict ledger

For each ongoing conflict track:

- parties;
- axis: goals, values, relationship, institution, scarcity, identity, knowledge, environment;
- stakes;
- current leverage;
- escalation ceiling;
- next reversible step;
- next irreversible step;
- payoff promise;
- deadline / intended horizon.

Escalate by **cost, consequence, choice and changed relationships**, not only by bigger enemies or larger explosions.

## Arc card

For each arc define:

- setup;
- promise / question;
- first turn;
- midpoint or revelation;
- reversal;
- climax;
- irreversible consequence;
- threads closed;
- threads intentionally carried forward;
- new state entering the next arc.

## Open-loop budget

Keep a visible list of mysteries, debts, promises and unresolved conflicts. Every new loop must have an intended payoff horizon or a reason to remain permanently unknowable.

## Flexibility

Use “tentpoles + flexible corridors”:

- tentpoles: required character choices, revelations, losses, alliances, endpoint;
- corridors: which episode, location, antagonist tactic, subplot, or route gets there.

This allows production discoveries without losing structural coherence.

## Common failures

- status-quo reset after every arc;
- mystery accumulation without payoff;
- power creep replacing dramatic complexity;
- fake deaths and reversible consequences becoming routine;
- crossover dependency for essential understanding;
- overplanning dozens of installments so tightly that characters cannot respond to discoveries.

Use **designing-character-systems** for ensemble evolution and **auditing-lore-continuity** before publication.
