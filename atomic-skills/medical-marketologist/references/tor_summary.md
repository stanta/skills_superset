# TOR summary: medical website promotion best practices

Derived from [`healthpilot/TOR_Medical_Promotion_Best_Practices.md`](healthpilot/TOR_Medical_Promotion_Best_Practices.md:1).

## Scope

- Primary domain: telemedicine, digital health platforms, AI health assistants
- Secondary domain: private clinics, specialized services (endocrinology, nutrition, longevity)

## Markets

- Tier 1 (innovation leaders): USA, UK, Israel
- Tier 2 (target markets): Russia & CIS, India, SEA

## Modules A–D (what to analyze/design)

### A) Trust & credibility

- Present clinicians with photos, bios, video intros, credential verification
- Handle social proof carefully (reviews, testimonials, case studies)
- Show relevant certifications/partnerships
- Communicate privacy and security clearly

### B) Content strategy & SEO

- Compare symptom-based vs solution-based content for high-intent traffic
- Use education hubs / encyclopedias in the funnel
- Use lead magnets (symptom checkers, BMI calculators, risk assessments)
- Use video to explain complex topics

### C) CRO (patient journey)

- Map landing → booking journeys
- Optimize primary CTAs (book, free consult, check symptoms)
- Design intake (chatbot vs form vs call)
- Present pricing transparently (bundles/subscriptions/pay-per-visit)
- Use ethical retargeting for drop-offs

### D) Compliance & ethics

- Identify Google Ads / Meta Ads red lines
- Frame results without unverified medical claims
- Place disclaimers appropriately

## Compliance framing patterns (from TOR)

- Prefer “wellness / lifestyle optimization” hooks to stay within ad policy constraints, then move users into private consultation flows.
- Prefer educational retargeting and avoid condition-revealing ads.
